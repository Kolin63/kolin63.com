This was a very unique experience for me--getting to work on a four-person team on a game over the timespan of 12 hours. Also, winning 2nd place in the global competition was a lot of fun and huge honor for us!

Pre-Event

I found out about Counterspell in the fall of 2024 when an organizer from Hack Club reached out to the advisor of my high school's computer club. I had known about Hack Club, the non-profit organization for high schoolers, for some time, but wasn't able to participate in any events until I became a freshman. So, when the announcement got sent out to the Computer Club, I was excited to jump at the opportunity!

With a few weeks until the event, I coordinated car rides with my friend Ayan, who I was going to team with. Also, I began to dust off my Godot skills so I could be ready for the 12-hour day.

The Event

Ayan and I woke up early and made our way to Fort Lee, about a 40 minute drive. We didn't know the theme of the jam yet, but I was able to give Ayan a quick run down of how to use Godot. Upon arriving, the two of us got our free T-Shirts and stickers, then picked a table to set up at. Not long after, we met Ayan Bindal and Hayden Lee, who we decided to merge groups with.

Just as we were moving to their table, the theme got announced: You Are Your Own Enemy. Hayden and Ayan Bindal already had an idea to make a plinko gambling game (a.k.a ball and pegs). We all liked it, so that's what we ran with.

I ended up doing most of the coding, while Ayan Bindal did the story writing, Hayden did the art, and Ayan Desai did the music. Working with my three friends on this was a great experience for me and taught me a lot about writing code quickly and collaborating with others.

By the end of the event, Bets and Regrets was very buggy and not that fun. For that reason, we scored very low. However, we were all able to see a vision for how to finish it up. The local competition was over, but Counterspell would have global submissions a week later.

Post Event

I took charge of organizing our work once the weekend was over. This included making a list of bugs, feature requests, and distributing the work load.

With whatever free time I had between school and band, I was able to get a lot of work done. By the end of the week, bugs were patched, the story was clearer, and the whole game felt more polished.

A few weeks later, an email was sent out, informing us that we had made the top 10 global submissions! From that point forth, the public would be able to vote on the top 10, and the most voted would win.

That was already enough accomplishment for the four of us, so I'm sure you can imagine our surprise when we placed second overall. Winning this was a huge honor for us, and it really put into perspective how a lot of hard work can pay off nicely.

Also, we were all sent Bambu Lab A1 Mini printers as a reward!

If you would like to play the game, you can do so on Itch.
