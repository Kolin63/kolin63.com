Also, I was thinking a bit about Endian.

So, my very original plan was to use a Goldberg polyhedron to represent the planets. That way, it could have a hex grid (besides 12 pentagons), and as we know, hexagons are the bestagons.

The problem with that was the 12 pentagons. Very infuriating. So, I decided to instead use a square tile grid on cube planets, where the planets are divided into 6 sectors (the faces of the cube). This has the added benefit of being far easier to represent in a text-based environment.

Now that I think of it, the text based part is actually the main reason I switched from Goldberg Polyhedra.

Ok, but now I'm thinking: maybe I shouldn't have a tile system at all!

The main reason I'm thinking this is because of the gameplay. Especially if you're on your phone, looking at an ASCII tile grid and figuring out what coordinates you want to move to would be a pain. Also, planets are big, so there would be a lot of tiles, and most of them would probably be useless.

My new idea is to use a POI system. There would be different types--or filters, depending on how I want to structure it--of POI's; for example, cities, ore deposits, forests, and space stations.

Each POI would have a DMS position, rather than a position locked on a tile grid. I guess, the DMS position is still a grid, but a much less limiting one.

Then, you can list out the nearby POI's and decide where you want to move to!

Really the main point of this switch is reducing entropy-- being able to find what you want quicker and easier.

Also, I mentioned DMS. Of course, that is only relevant to Earth. I'm not sure how I'll handle that on other planets for now. I'll probably just have a position struct that calls the fields DMS, but does not necesarilly mean Earth coordinates.

Then, I can have the numbers mean different things on different planets. Probably the amount of degrees in a circumference would be relative to the length of the circumference. So Jupiter would have a lot more degrees in its circumference.

I guess before I do that, I should check if there is already a system invented by the scientists!
